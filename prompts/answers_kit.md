## Write these sections (Markdown, with these headings)
### Self-introduction: data platform roles (100-150 words)
Current role; the single strongest piece of evidence with its scale; one line of trajectory;
what the candidate wants next. Lead with evidence, not chronology. End with a placeholder
sentence "[tie to this employer]".
### Self-introduction: data engineering / analytics roles (100-150 words)
Same structure, framed for warehouse/modelling/pipeline roles.
### Project entries
For each of the 4 strongest achievements: a descriptive project name (not the employer's
name), the candidate's role on it, a 60-word version and a 150-word version (what the system
did and for whom, the hardest problem and how it was solved, the outcome).
### Short pitches
Three versions under 140 characters each, each built on a different specific, true fact.
### Work authorisation answers (the most common automatic rejection is a knockout question)
Truthful, positively framed answers to "Are you authorised to work in X?" / "Will you now or in the
future require sponsorship?", one per market: Germany and other EU Blue Card countries (eligible for
the EU Blue Card; no employer sponsor licence needed; the candidate handles the application), the
Netherlands (highly skilled migrant route via a recognised sponsor), the UK (Skilled Worker visa via
a licensed sponsor), Ireland (Critical Skills Employment Permit), UAE/Singapore/Australia (employer-
sponsored work visa), India (no sponsorship needed). Never suggest answering untruthfully: say yes to
"require sponsorship" where it is true, and use the free-text field, if any, to make it easy.
### Common questions
Short answers (under 80 words each) for: why are you looking to move; notice period and
earliest start; work authorisation and sponsorship; willingness to relocate; expected salary
(placeholder brackets for the candidate to fill); what are you looking for in your next team.
Use the profile's `application_facts`; write "[fill in]" where a fact is missing.
