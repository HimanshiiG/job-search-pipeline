# How we run the job search (practice, not code)

Distilled 2026-11-08 from the two most-starred open-source job-search projects, read for their
reasoning rather than their code: career-ops (manifesto, FAQ, ethical-use rules, evaluation
blocks, profile template, warm-intro guide) and ai-job-search (README, evaluation framework,
writing style, calibration loop, verification checklist). Where they agree with our own
evidence (the candidate's own best letters), that is noted.

## 1. Apply better to fewer
- "Ten applications you believe in beat two hundred you don't." (career-ops manifesto)
- ai-job-search author: 69 tailored applications → 20 first interviews (~29%) → 1 job.
- Volume without tailoring just makes noise faster and spends the candidate's reputation.
- **For us:** discovery stays lenient (match ≥ 50%), but effort goes only into the day's top
  few, each tailored. The rest is a pool, not a to-do list.

## 2. Evidence over keywords
- Reformulate, never fabricate. Every claim and number traces to the resume/profile.
- Every requirement the JD states is answered: matched, or an honest bridge for a gap
  ("not in my daily toolkit yet; a natural extension of X"). Never silently omitted.
- Use the JD's own term where it is truthfully applicable; never keyword-stuff.
- **Interview backtrack test:** could the candidate explain this bullet in an interview without
  "what I actually meant was..."? If not, soften or drop it.
- Our Rules 2-4 (no gap-list claims, name the biggest gap, employer confidentiality) are the same idea.

## 3. Know what each employer is buying
- Define **archetypes**: for each role type, what it buys and which proof points lead.
- An **exit narrative** (why move, why now, why there) frames every document.
- A **signature advantage**: the thing this candidate does that others don't.
- **Six-second gate:** the resume's top third and the letter's opening make the target role,
  strongest matching stack and one production outcome impossible to miss.
- **Level:** sell the level you can honestly defend; know what you'd accept if down-levelled.

## 4. Eligibility and legitimacy before effort
- Eligibility first. "Silence is not permission." Citizenship, permanent residency or security
  clearance requirements are a hard stop.
- Is the posting real and open? Closed banners, reposts of the same role, vague or contradictory
  JDs, layoffs in that team: signals, never accusations; the human weighs them.
- Never apply to the same role twice through an agency and directly.

## 5. Write forward, about their problem
- Open on something specific the company published (blog, case study, talk, the JD's own
  opinionated sentence). Prefer a fetched page; never an unverified claim.
- Which of their problems you solve and how; 1-2 past results as proof; motivation early.
- Name the biggest gap honestly. 350-420 words. No clichés ("passionate", "leverage",
  "hit the ground running", "excited", "data-driven"), no em-dashes, no apologetic hedging.
- Business-value bullets: action + system/scope + tool + outcome + proof.

## 6. Talk to people
- Warm intros first: people you already know at the company (LinkedIn connections export).
- Then one well-chosen person: hiring manager > recruiter > team peer. One short message shaped
  to that person (recruiter: fit + proof that pre-answers screening; hiring manager: their
  challenge + your best result on it; peer: genuine interest in their work, never ask for a job).
- LinkedIn connection notes: 200 characters on a free account, 300 on Premium.
- Call the employer only with a substantive question about the role, never "to be remembered";
  then reference the conversation in the letter.
- Respect recruiters' time. Drafts only; the human sends.

## 7. Close the loop
- Record every outcome. Follow up after 10 quiet days, at most twice, using only claims already sent.
- After enough results, check whether scores predicted interviews and recalibrate targeting.
- After a run of noes, name the recurring gap and plan to learn it (our backlog: Terraform, Airflow).

## 8. The profile is a living document
- Output quality depends on profile depth: what you did, with what, at what scale, with what result.
- Any fact that surfaces later goes back into the profile the same day, or later drafts will treat
  it as unsupported.

## 9. A human decides
- Nothing is ever submitted automatically. The tool prepares; the person chooses.
- Be honest with employers about using AI if asked; it tends to start a real conversation.
